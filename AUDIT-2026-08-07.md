# Augzet V2 — Website Audit

**Date:** 7 August 2026
**Audited against:** Faith Technologies, Sterling & Wilson, Rosendin
**Method:** live DOM/computed-style measurement at 1440×900 and 375×812, full asset inventory, benchmark transcription

---

## Verdict

The site is well-built and badly aimed.

The craft is real — a coherent token system, honest copy, no invented data, working responsive behaviour, no horizontal overflow at 375px. That is more than most contractor sites in Kerala have.

But it fails the only test that matters. **A visitor cannot tell what Augzet does, who it is for, or what they get, in five seconds** — and the single largest visual on the page actively sells the wrong service. On top of that, the primary conversion path is broken: the quote form submits nowhere.

Three things are true at once:

1. **The hero contradicts the positioning.** `hero.mp4` is a 6.8 MB drone shot of a solar farm. The client's own July pushback was *"one of our services is solar, the core is electrical."* The biggest asset on the site says the opposite.
2. **The form is dead.** `contact.html` has no `action`. Every CTA on all nine pages funnels into a form that discards the lead.
3. **The design system quietly collapsed.** `--gold` was remapped to `--red`, so the two-tone system the whole visual language was built on now renders as one loud red — taking primary/secondary CTA hierarchy and five accessibility-safe chips down with it.

Everything else in this document is downstream of those three.

## Part 0 — What the benchmarks actually taught us

I could read Faith Technologies, Sterling & Wilson and Rosendin. **Cupertino Electric would not render** — it is fully client-side and blocks automated fetches, so nothing in this document is attributed to it. Rosendin substitutes as the third US electrical-contractor reference.

### Where they are the standard

| Signal | Faith | Sterling & Wilson | Rosendin | Augzet V2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proof is third-party verifiable | project case studies | 26+ awards, ISO, govt clients | ENR #3, Forbes #156, founded 1919 | 21 Google reviews, self-asserted stats |
| Named marquee projects | 5 named | Supreme Court, state data centres | SoFi Stadium, CLT Airport, UCSF | 1 named ("M/s. Sunny and Sunnys") |
| Markets/sectors as an axis | Markets Served | — | 11 sectors listed | none |
| Homepage restraint | 4 sections | 5 sections | 6 sections | **13 sections** |
| Careers as a first-class nav item | yes | yes | yes | no |

Three lessons worth stealing:

- **Sector segmentation.** Rosendin lists 11 markets; Faith has "Markets Served" as a top-level axis. Augzet has four *services* and zero *sectors*. A villa owner and a factory owner are being sold the identical page. Sector is the missing axis.
- **Proof must be external.** All three lead with things a stranger can verify — an ENR ranking, an award year, a named stadium. Augzet's "186+ projects / 1,000+ kW / 1,00,000 kg CO₂" are unverifiable self-claims presented in the biggest type on the page. The 21 Google reviews are the *only* externally checkable proof on the site and they are buried at section 11 of 13.
- **Restraint.** None of the three runs more than six homepage sections. Augzet runs thirteen, and two of them (`#credentials`, `#commitments`) are the *same four-cell component* saying overlapping things.

### Where copying them would be a mistake

This matters more than the above, and it is the part the generic prompt got wrong.

Faith's hero is *"Leading with Energy."* Sterling & Wilson's is *"Redefining 21st century engineering."* Rosendin's is *"Building Quality. Building Value. Building People."* All three are content-free.

**They can afford that. Augzet cannot.** Those firms win nine-figure work through RFPs, procurement relationships and ENR rankings; their website is a credibility backstop for someone who already knows the name. Augzet's visitor is a homeowner in Ernakulam or an SME building owner who found them on Google Maps, has never heard of them, and is deciding whether to fill in a form or close the tab.

Augzet's competitor is not Sterling & Wilson. **It is the visitor doing nothing for another eight months.**

So: benchmark the three for *craft* — information architecture, proof density, photographic restraint, typographic discipline. Do **not** benchmark them for hero strategy. Augzet's hero has to do a job theirs doesn't.

## Part 2 — Hormozi value equation, scored

> Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

Scoring the current homepage 0–10 on each term.

### Dream Outcome — 3/10

The site sells **process**, not outcome. Count the section headings: "Designed on paper. Built to last.", "Qualified to sign the drawing.", "Simulation-First Engineering", "The drawing comes first."

That is four separate sections about *how carefully Augzet documents things*. Documentation is not a dream outcome. It is a feature, and worse, a feature the buyer cannot evaluate.

What the customer actually wants, in their own words: a lower electricity bill, a house where nothing trips, lights and AC they control from a phone, a system that doesn't need a different guy every time it breaks, and the MNRE subsidy actually landing in their account.

**The site has all of these buried in body copy and none of them in a heading.** The strongest outcome claim on the entire page — *subsidy credited in 40 days* — appears as beat 03 of a sticky case study at roughly 70% scroll depth, and as a review headline at 85%.

### Perceived Likelihood of Achievement — 5/10

Assets present: 21 Google reviews rated Excellent, three verbatim review quotes with real names, KSEB Supervisor Licence, three B.Tech founders, MNRE registration, one named flagship client.

What undermines it:

- The three founders are represented by **`avatar.png` ×3** — the same generic silhouette repeated. On a page whose argument is "the people who design it are the people who install it," anonymous grey avatars are the single most damaging image on the site.
- The stats band leads with **unverifiable self-claims** (186+, 1,000+ kW, 10,000+ kg CO₂) in 63px type, while the one externally checkable number (21 Google reviews) sits in the fourth slot at the same weight.
- Two capability tiles still render **Unsplash stock**.
- No guarantee. No warranty terms. No AMC terms. No "if X doesn't happen, we do Y."

### Time Delay — 3/10

Time delay is the gap between paying and getting the outcome, *and* the visitor's perception of how long the whole thing will take.

**Correction to an earlier draft of this section:** I first wrote that the site never states a timeline. That is wrong. `contact.html` commits to *"a response within one business day"* three times — in the page lede, in the success panel, and in the "What Happens Next" aside. The service detail pages carry a four-step process with real sequencing.

The problem is placement, not absence. **Every timeline commitment on the site lives on pages a visitor only reaches after they have already decided to enquire.** The homepage — the page that has to do the convincing — states no timeline at all. The only number that surfaces there is "40 days" for one client's subsidy, at 70% scroll depth, framed as a past anecdote rather than an expectation.

Compounding it: **the site itself is slow.** 6.82 MB of autoplay video before anything else loads. On a Kerala 4G connection that is 8–15 seconds to first meaningful paint. A visitor who came to find out if this is worth doing has already spent ten seconds finding out nothing.

### Effort & Sacrifice — 4/10

The "one point of contact / one team designs, installs and maintains" argument is genuinely good and genuinely differentiating. It is the strongest thing on the site.

It is also stated for the first time at **section 9 of 13**, inside a four-cell grid identical to the four-cell grid three sections earlier.

Working against it:

- **The quote form does not submit.** `<form>` has no `action` attribute and defaults to `method="get"`. A visitor who does everything right gets a fake success message and Augzet never hears from them.
- The form asks for seven fields including "Avg. monthly electricity bill" — a question a cold visitor may not know and may not want to answer.
- **On mobile the header CTA is hidden entirely** (`.header-cta { display: none }` at ≤720px). For a local contractor whose customers phone, there is no tap-to-call anywhere except an 18px-tall link in the footer, 22 screens down.

### Score summary

| Term | Score | Single biggest lever |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | 3/10 | Put an outcome in the H1, not a metaphor |
| Likelihood | 5/10 | Real founder photos; lead with the reviews; add a guarantee |
| Time Delay | 3/10 | State the timeline; cut the 6.8 MB video |
| Effort & Sacrifice | 4/10 | Fix the form; sticky call bar on mobile; move "one contact" up |

The bottom half of the equation is where the cheapest wins are, exactly as briefed. **Fixing the form, cutting the video and adding a mobile call bar are all one-day jobs and all three multiply everything above them.**

## Part 3 — Selling against inaction

The site currently makes zero argument against doing nothing. There is no cost-of-delay, no risk reversal, no urgency that isn't manufactured.

Real, honest levers available to this business:

| Lever | Why it is true for Augzet | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| **The bill is the clock** | Every month without solar is a bill paid to KSEB that a system would have offset. This is arithmetic, not pressure. | Hero sub-proof or a simple payback strip |
| **Subsidy is finite and procedural** | MNRE rooftop subsidy has budget cycles and paperwork lead time. Augzet is registered to process it — 40 days on record. | A dedicated proof block, high on the page |
| **Retrofit costs more than design** | Automation and monitoring designed into the wiring cost a fraction of retrofitting them later. This is Augzet's actual integration argument. | The integration section, rewritten |
| **The doubt is "will they disappear after install?"** | This is the real objection in Kerala contracting, and Augzet's in-house AMC answers it directly. | Guarantee block, above the form |

### The guarantee gap

Not one promise on the site is falsifiable. "Honest, certified, on time" is a value, not a guarantee. Compare a real one:

> *Fixed quote. If the final invoice exceeds the quoted figure for the specified scope, we absorb the difference.*

That is a claim that costs Augzet something if broken, which is exactly why it converts. **Three of the four Google reviews independently mention "no hidden charges" and "as per the quotation" — the client is already delivering this. They are just not promising it.**

Turning an existing behaviour into an explicit guarantee is free. This is the highest-leverage copy change on the site.

**[CONFIRM with client]** — any guarantee wording must be signed off before it ships. Do not invent warranty periods, response times or AMC terms.

## Part 4 — Craft defects (measured)

### 4.1 The design system collapsed — CRITICAL

`assets/css/main.css:20-21`:

```css
--gold:   #CD0A1B;  /* Mapped to Red (Black, White & Red only) */
--gold-2: #B3000E;  /* Mapped to Darker Red */
```

`--gold` and `--red` are now the same value. Every two-tone relationship in the system silently became monotone. Measured consequences:

| Component | Designed behaviour | Actual behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `.chip` / `.chip--red` | gold on dark, red on light | 11 chips, all identical red |
| `.btn-bar` / `.btn-bar--red` | gold secondary, red primary | Hero's two CTAs are the same bar. **No primary/secondary hierarchy.** |
| `.t-gold` accent spans | gold half of a two-tone headline | 7 spans, all red |
| `.gap-tile--join` | red border marks the integration point among gold tiles | All three tiles identical. The "join" concept is invisible. |
| `.vrule` / `.vrule--red` | two rule colours | one |
| `.form-success` | — | still hardcoded `rgba(240,148,29,.06)` — orphan orange from the old gold |

**This one variable is responsible for more visual damage than any other line in the codebase.** It also caused a hard accessibility failure:

### 4.2 Chip contrast fails WCAG AA — CRITICAL

`.chip` sets `color: var(--charcoal-2)` on `background: var(--gold)`. That was legible on gold. On red it is **#221F1F on #CD0A1B = 3.10:1**, against a 4.5:1 requirement at 13px.

Five chips currently fail: `OUR CAPABILITIES`, `THE RECORD`, `CREDENTIALS`, `FLAGSHIP DELIVERY`, `TALK TO AN ELECTRICAL ENGINEER`.

The other six use `.chip--red` (white on red, 5.2:1) and pass. **The two chip variants are visually identical and one of them is broken.**

### 4.3 No focus states — CRITICAL

The entire stylesheet contains **one** `:focus` rule (`.field input/select/textarea`) and **zero** `:focus-visible` rules. Every link, button, nav item, filter and CTA on all nine pages falls back to the browser default ring — over a dark charcoal header and red surfaces, effectively invisible.

WCAG 2.4.7. Straightforward fix, affects every page.

### 4.4 Typography is 2× the benchmark scale

Measured at 1440px:

| Element | Rendered | Style |
|---|---|---|
| h1 | 70.6px | uppercase, 7.06px tracking |
| h2 | 47.5px | uppercase, 4.75px tracking |
| h3 | 28.1px | uppercase, 1.97px tracking |
| body | 15.8px | line-height 1.8 |

**29 of 31 headings on the homepage are uppercase.** None of the three benchmarks does this — all reserve uppercase for eyebrows and labels, and set headings in sentence case.

The root cause is documented in the CSS itself: the scale was designed for **Bebas Neue**, a condensed display face. The site now loads **Oxygen**, a humanist sans. Uppercase Oxygen at 0.1em tracking occupies roughly 40% more horizontal space than condensed at the same point size — which is the entire "fonts are extremely large / looks zoomed in" complaint, still unresolved.

`README.md:33` still documents Bebas Neue / Oswald / Montserrat. It is stale.

### 4.5 Homepage length and redundancy

- Desktop: **14,823px = 16.5 screens.** Mobile: **18,495px = 22.8 screens.**
- **13 body sections.** Faith runs 4, Sterling & Wilson 5, Rosendin 6.
- `#credentials` and `#commitments` are the **same `.why-grid` component**, four cells each, separated by one section.
- Five sections make overlapping versions of the same argument: `#company`, `#integration`, `#discipline`, `#credentials`, `#commitments`.
- Heading order skips a level: `H1 → H3` in `#company`.

### 4.6 Performance

| Asset | Size | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `hero.mp4` | 6.82 MB | autoplay, blocks LCP |
| `hero-poster.jpg` | 273 KB | |
| 3 unused `.mp4` files | 15.2 MB | shipping in the deploy folder, referenced nowhere |
| `logo.png` | 768px natural, rendered 152px | 5× oversampled, PNG not SVG |

**22 MB of video in a folder that serves 6.8 MB of it on first paint.**

### 4.7 Mobile

| Issue | Detail |
|---|---|
| No header CTA | `.header-cta { display: none }` at ≤720px — no visible quote or call action |
| No tap-to-call | phone appears only in pre-footer and footer, as 17–18px links |

## Part 7 — Page-by-page

### 7.1 The headline finding

**The homepage is the weakest page on the site, and the four service detail pages are the strongest.**

That is backwards, and it explains a lot. The service pages contain the best copy Augzet has:

> *"Most installers quote from a satellite photo. We model."*

That single line does more competitive positioning than all thirteen homepage sections combined. It names the competitor's behaviour, names Augzet's, and lets the reader draw the conclusion. It is on `services/solar-power.html`, four clicks and 16 screens away from where a cold visitor lands.

The service pages also carry the only **FAQs** on the site, and the only honest hedging anywhere:

> *"Our fastest was credited 40 days after commissioning. That is not a guarantee (processing times vary)…"*

That hedge is worth more than the claim. It is the single most trust-building sentence on the website and it appears on one page.

**Implication for the rebuild:** the homepage's job is not to say everything. It is to make the value prop land in five seconds and route people into these pages fast. Cutting it from 13 sections to 6 is not a loss of content — the content already exists, better written, one level down.

### 7.2 `services.html` — the best-structured page

Three content sections: capability grid → 4-step method → CTA. Restrained, benchmark-comparable, clean semantics (`<ul>`/`<li>` for the grid).

| Issue | Severity |
|---|---|
| One Unsplash placeholder (Testing & Maintenance panel) | High |
| `.section--fullbleed-mobile` — class used once, **zero CSS defined** | Low (dead) |
| `.page-hero-rule` — **zero CSS defined** (see §8.1) | Medium |
| Capability descriptions differ from the homepage's for the same four services | Medium — two sources of truth |

### 7.3 `projects.html` — the proof-integrity problem

This is the page a sceptical buyer goes to, and it is the page least able to withstand scrutiny.

**H1: "186 projects. Zero shortcuts."** — the largest type on the page asserts a number nothing on the site substantiates.

Of five project cards, **one is a real, named job**: the 80 kWp on-grid plant for M/s. Sunny and Sunnys, with named hardware (Renewsys modules, Growatt inverters). That card is excellent.

The other four are what the README calls "representative profiles" — and they carry **invented specifics**:

| Card | Fabricated detail |
|---|---|
| Full Electrical Design, New Villa Build | "Load: **42 kVA**", "2D + SLD" |
| IoT Automation, Green Villa Project | "Nodes: **24**" |
| Annual Maintenance Portfolio | "Response: Priority" |
| Rooftop Plants Across a Villa Development | "Scope: Multi-roof" |

42 kVA and 24 nodes are presented in the same `.proj-stats` component, in the same typeface, at the same weight, as the real 80 kWp figure. A visitor cannot tell which is which. **This is the same class of problem as the Kalyan spec sheet** — written copy occupying a slot the reader parses as data.

If a prospect asks "which villa was the 42 kVA job?", there is no answer. That is a worse outcome than having four fewer cards.

**Recommendation:** cut to the real jobs. One named, verifiable project beats five where four are illustrative. If the client supplies real figures for the others, restore them.

**Also on this page:**

- `role="tablist"` on the filter bar, with plain `<button>` children carrying **no `role="tab"`, no `aria-selected`, no `aria-controls`, and no `tabpanel`**. A screen reader announces a tab list with zero tabs. This is worse than omitting the role entirely.
- Filtered-out cards use `display: none` with no live-region announcement — a screen-reader user gets no feedback that the result set changed.
- One Unsplash placeholder.

### 7.5 The four service detail pages — strongest content, shared defects

All four share one template: page hero → full-bleed photo band → body + sticky aside → process → scale/case → FAQ → CTA. It is a good template.

**What works:** the sticky aside carries scope, deliverables, a CTA and a direct phone line — it converts while the reader reads. The FAQ blocks pre-handle objections. The copy is specific and confident.

**Shared defects:**

| Issue | Count | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| `.page-hero-rule` with zero CSS | 4 pages | Medium |
| `.eyebrow` failing contrast (see §8.2) | 12 instances | Critical |
| `style="color:var(--gold)"` inline on dark, failing contrast | 4 instances | Critical |
| FAQ present but **no `FAQPage` schema** | 4 pages | High (SEO) |

## Part 8 — Code integrity

### 8.1 Dead CSS classes shipping in the HTML

| Class | HTML uses | CSS rules |
|---|---|---|
| `.page-hero-rule` | **7 pages** | **0** |
| `.section--fullbleed-mobile` | 1 | **0** |

`.page-hero-rule` is the gold vertical accent rule — the signature device that anchors every inner-page hero. The stylesheet defines `.page-hero .vrule-inline` instead. **Nobody ever noticed it wasn't rendering**, which is itself a finding: the accent is either load-bearing (in which case fix the class) or it isn't (in which case delete the markup). Currently it is neither.

### 8.2 Contrast failures sitewide — the full count

The `--gold: #CD0A1B` remap (§4.1) breaks every rule that pairs dark text with a gold background. Full sitewide count:

| Element | Instances | Contrast | Required |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.chip` (index) | 5 | 3.10:1 | 4.5:1 |
| `.eyebrow` (8 other pages) | 20 | 3.10:1 | 4.5:1 |
| `style="color:var(--gold)"` on dark | 8 | 3.10:1 | 4.5:1 |
| **Total** | **33** | | |

**Thirty-three elements across nine pages fail WCAG AA because of one variable.** Restoring `#F0941D` fixes all 33 at once: gold on charcoal-2 measures **7.60:1**, which clears AAA.

⚠️ **One trap in the revert:** gold on the light ground (`#F0941D` on `#F4F4F4`) is **2.13:1** — a worse failure than the one being fixed. The homepage has `.t-gold` spans inside `.section--concrete` (`#integration`, `#team`), and `about.html` has four more. The revert **must** ship with a companion rule forcing `.t-gold` to red on light sections. The original design intended exactly this ("white → gold on dark, all-red on light"); the rule was simply never written.

### 8.3 The form actively lies — CRITICAL

Worse than the dead `action` reported in Part 1. Three defects compound:

1. `contact.html:58` — `<form class="quote-form" novalidate>`. **`novalidate` disables browser validation**, so every `required` attribute on the form is inert.
2. No `action`, `method` defaults to `get`.
3. `main.js:250-262` — `initForm()` calls `e.preventDefault()`, reveals `.form-success`, hides the form, and scrolls to it.

Combined behaviour: **a visitor can submit a completely empty form and be told:**

> RECEIVED. AN ENGINEER REVIEWS EVERY REQUEST. EXPECT A RESPONSE WITHIN ONE BUSINESS DAY.

The site issues a false confirmation. A real prospect walks away believing Augzet has their details and is working on it. They wait a day, hear nothing, and conclude Augzet is unprofessional — the exact opposite of everything the copy argues.

This is the single most damaging defect on the website. It is worse than having no form at all.

### 8.4 Dead JavaScript

`main.js` animates five selectors that no longer exist anywhere in the HTML — leftovers from the SVG hero that the video replaced:

`#hero-sun` · `#hero-glow` · `#hero-haze` · `#hero-glint` · `.manifesto-bg-sun`

Roughly 40 lines of dead animation code, all guarded by null checks so nothing breaks — it is inert weight, not a bug.

Separately, `main.js:137` listens for a custom `projectsRendered` event that **nothing in the codebase dispatches** (it belongs to the Sanity integration in `static/`). Consequence: the `.proj-grid` reveal animation never runs. Cards are visible — `gsap.from` was never applied — so this degrades silently rather than breaking.

### 8.5 Mobile menu accessibility

The overlay menu opens and sets `body { overflow: hidden }`, but:

- **No focus trap.** Tab order continues through the page behind the overlay.
- **Focus is never moved into the menu** on open, or returned to the toggle on close.
- **Escape does not close it.**
- Background content is not `inert` / `aria-hidden`.

On the site's primary breakpoint, the primary navigation is keyboard-inaccessible in practice.

### 8.6 Deploy hygiene

`.gitignore` correctly excludes `node_modules/`, the WhatsApp archives and the raw photo drop. But:

- **`static/` is tracked — 24 files, 6.6 MB** of compiled Sanity Studio vendor bundles (`refractor`, `SanityVision`, `stegaEncodeSourceMap`…). No page references them. Dead weight in the deploy.
- **No `sitemap.xml`, no `robots.txt`.**

## Part 11 — Photography direction

The client shoots on a phone, in daylight, unposed. That is not a limitation to hide — it is the honest register for a fifteen-person Ernakulam contractor, and it will out-convert polished stock every time. The direction below leans into it rather than fighting it.

**House style:**

| Parameter | Direction |
|---|---|
| Register | Documentary. Working photographs, not portraits of work. |
| Light | Natural only. Golden hour or overcast. **No flash, no HDR.** |
| Lens | 24–35mm equivalent. Phone-native. Avoid ultra-wide distortion on faces. |
| Camera height | Eye level or slightly below for site work — it reads as *present*, not *supervising*. |
| Depth of field | Deep. Everything sharp. Portrait-mode blur reads as fake and undercuts the documentary register. |
| Colour grade | Neutral to slightly cool. Kerala midday runs green from foliage — pull green, protect skin tones. **No orange-and-teal.** |
| Wardrobe | Augzet-branded polo where possible (already visible in p01, p09, p13). Hard hat and hi-vis where the site requires it — never as costume. |
| Expression | Working, not smiling at camera. The award-ceremony and group shots are the exception and belong on the culture/careers page only. |
| Framing | Leave headroom in the upper third — every hero and photo-band overlays text there. |
| Editing | Straighten verticals. Crop tight. **No filters, no vignettes, no watermarks.** |

**The reference shot is p13** — engineer, hard hat, drawing in hand, live site, workers mid-task behind, late-afternoon light, deep focus, no eye contact with camera. Every future photo request should cite it.

**Shot list to request:**

1. Hands at an open distribution board, terminal-level, natural light — the electrical hero
2. Engineer with a laptop showing a simulation, on site rather than at a desk
3. Meter/instrument in use during testing — clamp meter, insulation tester, anything with a readable display
4. Automation panel or wall control being demonstrated to a client in a finished room
5. A completed villa exterior at dusk, lights on, shot by them (currently the only dusk shot is `villa-night-lit.jpg`, already used three times)
6. Three founder headshots — same wall, same light, same crop, shoulders up

## Part 12 — Illustration and diagram direction

Photography cannot carry the integration argument. That is the correct diagnosis behind the "The join" problem: a photograph of inverter internals is unreadable to a homeowner because **it depicts a thing, and the argument is about a relationship.**

**One diagram style, applied everywhere:**

| Parameter | Spec |
|---|---|
| Register | Single-line electrical schematic. Augzet's own professional language, used as brand. |
| Stroke | Uniform 2px. No weight variation, no tapering. |
| Corners | Hard 90°. No rounded joins. Matches the zero-radius UI. |
| Fill | None. Line only, on charcoal or light ground. |
| Colour | Charcoal/white for structure. **Gold for the energy path. Red for the single point being argued.** Restores a real job for the two-tone system. |
| Type | Oxygen caps, 0.2em tracking, 12px — same as `.mono-meta` |
| Motion | Optional single dash-offset animation along the energy path. Nothing else moves. |
| Never | 3D, isometric, gradients, drop shadows, illustrated people, generic flat-vector icons |

**Diagram 1 — the integration schematic** (replaces the Electrical / The join / Solar photo strip)

Grid supply, solar array, and automation controller all drawn converging on a single distribution board, with loads leaving the other side. The DB is the only element in red. One caption: *"Everything meets at the board. We design the board."*

This states the entire company thesis in one image, keeps electrical structurally central, and cannot be mistaken for a photograph of someone else's job.

**Diagram 2 — four capability marks.** One schematic glyph per service, same stroke weight, drawn from real symbols: DB and circuit for design, controller node and branch for automation, PV cell and inverter for solar, meter and probe for testing. Replaces the four photo crops in `cap2-media` and kills the Unsplash tile.

**Diagram 3 — bill-offset chart.** Two stacked bars, before and after. Gold for solar generation, charcoal for grid draw. Axis labelled in ₹. Supports the cost-of-delay argument in §3.

⚠️ **Do not generate photographic imagery.** Diagrams are honest because nobody reads them as a record of a specific job. A generated photograph in a project slot is a claim about work that was never done — which is the line the current hero video crosses.

## Part 14 — Emotional experience

**What the site currently leaves you with: competent, careful, slightly cold, and faintly generic.**

The dominant impression is *thoroughness* — thirteen sections of it. A visitor comes away believing Augzet is organised and probably good at their job, but without a single memorable image, phrase or person attached to the name. A week later they would not recall the company.

Three specific causes:

1. **No faces.** The only humans on the homepage are three grey silhouettes. Every photograph is of equipment. The word "team" appears constantly; the site never shows one. Seven photographs of fifteen real people, and an award ceremony, are sitting unused in a zip file.
2. **Monotone red.** After the token collapse, every accent, chip, border, rule, arrow and headline highlight is the same alarm red. Sustained high-alarm colour with no relief reads as loud rather than confident. Restoring gold restores tonal range.
3. **Uppercase everything.** 29 of 31 homepage headings in tracked caps. Uppercase does not read as premium — it reads as *shouting at a consistent volume*, which flattens emphasis. When everything is emphasised, nothing is.

**What it should leave you with:** *these are careful people, and they are still here in five years.*

That is the emotional territory the business actually owns — the Google reviews say it, the in-house AMC proves it, the fifteen-person team and the employee-award photo prove it. The site argues it in prose and never once shows it.

**Three changes that shift the emotional register more than any redesign:**

1. Real founders' faces, in that offset red corner-rule frame (§10)
2. The team photograph, captioned honestly — "the people who will be on your job"
3. Gold restored, uppercase relaxed
