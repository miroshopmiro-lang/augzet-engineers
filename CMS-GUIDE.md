# Augzet Engineers — Website Content Guide

**How to add and manage projects on your website.**
Prepared by Apollosystems · 8 August 2026

---

## Before you start — the one-minute version

Your website has a small control panel where you can **add, edit and remove the projects shown on your Projects page**. Nothing else on the site is editable from there, and nothing you do in the panel can break the rest of the website.

You do not need any technical knowledge. If you can fill in a form and upload a photo from your phone, you can do this.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Where** | `https://augzet.com/admin` *(confirmed at launch — see §1)* |
| **What you can change** | The project cards on the Projects page |
| **What you cannot change** | Every other page — home, services, about, contact |
| **How long changes take to appear** | Usually within a minute or two |
| **Can you break the site?** | No. Worst case, the page shows the older projects instead |

## 1. Getting in

**Address:** your website address followed by `/admin` — so `https://augzet.com/admin`.

> **Note for launch:** this address becomes active once the new site goes live. Until then, use the temporary address Apollosystems gives you. Bookmark whichever one applies.

**Logging in.** The panel is not open to the public. Apollosystems sends you an email invitation first — accept that invitation once, and after that you log in with the same email or Google account every time. You will not need a separate password to remember if you use the Google option.

If the login screen says you do not have access, the invitation has not been accepted yet, or you are signing in with a different email than the one invited. Tell Apollosystems which email address you want to use and it will be corrected.

**On a phone.** The panel works on a phone browser, but adding a project involves several fields and a photo upload. It is genuinely faster on a laptop. If you only have a phone, turn it sideways.

---

## 2. What you will see

After logging in you get a screen titled **Augzet Portal** with a single item in the left sidebar: **Projects**.

That is deliberate. The panel has been stripped down so there is nothing to click that you should not be clicking. Everything you need is under Projects.

Click **Projects** and you see the list of every project currently on your website, newest first. Each row shows the project photo, its title, and its category and date underneath.

---

## 3. Adding a new project

Click **Projects**, then the **+** (new document) button at the top of the list.

You get a form with six things to fill in. Fill them all — the panel will not let you publish until every required field is complete, and it will tell you exactly which one is missing.

### Project Title
The name of the job, as you would say it to a customer.

> **Example:** `5 kW Rooftop Plant, Aluva`

Include the location. It is the single most useful thing to a visitor deciding whether you work in their area.

**Rule: between 5 and 80 characters.** If you type a title that is too long the panel will show a red warning and refuse to publish. Shorten it and the warning disappears.

### Status
Two round buttons — pick one:

| Choose | For |
|---|---|
| **Completed** | The job is finished and handed over |
| **Ongoing** | Work is still in progress, or it is a running contract such as an AMC |

New projects default to **Completed**, so most of the time you can leave this alone.

**This matters more than it looks.** Your Projects page has filter buttons across the top — *All Work*, *Completed*, *Ongoing* — and this field is what decides which filter a project appears under.

**Remember to come back and change it.** A job added as *Ongoing* stays Ongoing until someone edits it. A page still showing a two-year-old project as "ongoing" does more damage than not listing it at all. Make it a habit: when you hand over a job, open the panel and flip it to Completed.

**Ongoing projects are worth showing.** A visitor who sees live work knows you are busy and working now. A page of only finished jobs cannot prove that.

### Price / Date
One short line of detail shown under the category on the card. Either a price or a date — whichever is more impressive for that job.

> **Examples:** `Rs. 4.5 Lakhs` · `December 2025` · `Completed March 2026`

**Rule: maximum 40 characters.**

**A word of advice on prices.** You are not obliged to show one. A visitor who sees a price they cannot afford leaves, and a visitor who sees no price enquires. A good pattern is to show dates for most projects and prices only on the ones that make you look affordable.

### Description
One or two sentences on what you actually delivered. Written for a customer, not an engineer.

> **Example:** `Complete rooftop solar installation for a residential client in Aluva, including net metering and MNRE subsidy processing. Commissioned and handed over in 40 days.`

**Rule: between 20 and 200 characters.** That is short on purpose — roughly two sentences. Long descriptions do not get read and they break the card layout.

**What makes a good one:** say what was done, where, and one thing that went well. Avoid "we are committed to excellence" — nobody has ever chosen a contractor because of that sentence.

### Project Image
Upload one photo. Click the field, choose the file, wait for the upload to finish.

This is the most important field on the form. A visitor looks at the photo before reading a single word.

**What to upload:**
- A photo of the **finished work**, taken in daylight
- Landscape (sideways) orientation, not portrait
- The actual job — not a stock photo, not a logo, not a screenshot
- At least 1200 pixels wide. Straight off a modern phone camera is fine

**What not to upload:** dark photos, photos with a person's face front and centre, WhatsApp-forwarded images (they get compressed and look poor blown up on a website), or a photo with another company's watermark on it.

**Setting the focal point.** After the photo uploads you will see a small **Hotspot** or crop option. Open it and drag the circle onto the part of the photo that matters — the panel, the array, the installation. Your website shows this photo at different shapes on different screen sizes, and the hotspot tells it what it must never crop out. Skip this and a phone visitor may see a photo cropped down to a patch of sky.

### Key Technical Stats
Optional, but they make a card look substantially more credible. Click **Add item** to add one. Each stat has a **Label** and a **Value**.

| Label | Value |
|---|---|
| `Capacity` | `5.0 kWp` |
| `Type` | `On-grid` |
| `Subsidy` | `MNRE` |

**Rules: maximum 3 stats. Label maximum 15 characters. Value maximum 20 characters.**

Three is the cap because the card is only so wide — a fourth would not fit. Use all three where you can; two look sparse.

## 4. Publishing

Nothing you type is on your website until you press **Publish** (bottom right).

Until then it is a draft — saved, safe, invisible to the public. You can leave a half-finished project as a draft for days and come back to it.

**After publishing:** your Projects page picks up the change within a minute or two. If you look immediately and do not see it, wait a moment and refresh the page properly — hold `Ctrl` and press `R` on Windows, or `Cmd` + `R` on a Mac.

---

## 5. Editing and removing

**To edit:** click the project in the list, change what you want, press **Publish** again. The old version is replaced.

**To remove a project from the website** you have two options, and the difference matters:

- **Unpublish** — takes it off the website but keeps everything in the panel. Use this for a project you may want back, or one where the client has asked you to take it down temporarily. This is almost always the right choice.
- **Delete** — removes it permanently. It cannot be recovered.

Both are under the **⋮** menu next to the Publish button.

**Prefer Unpublish.** There is no undo on Delete.

---

## 6. Things worth knowing

**Your website has a safety net.** If the panel is ever unreachable, or if you delete every single project, your Projects page automatically falls back to a built-in set of example project cards. Your website never shows an empty page and never shows an error to a visitor.

The consequence: **if you delete all your real projects, the old example cards reappear.** That is not a fault — it is the safety net doing its job. Add one real project back and it takes over again.

**Newest first.** Projects appear in the order they were created, most recent at the top. There is no drag-to-reorder. If you want a particular job showing first, the practical trick is to delete and re-add it, which makes it the newest.

**Only the Projects page is connected to the panel.** The homepage, services pages, about page and contact details are built into the site. To change those, message Apollosystems — they are quick changes, they are just not self-service.

**There is no limit** on how many projects you can add. But a Projects page with 8 strong projects converts better than one with 30 weak ones. Add your best work, and replace rather than accumulate.

## 7. If something looks wrong

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| A red warning under a field | Read it — it tells you the exact limit you have exceeded. Shorten the text and it clears |
| **Publish** button greyed out | A required field is empty, or a red warning is still showing somewhere on the form. Scroll up through the whole form |
| Published, but not on the website | Wait two minutes, then hard-refresh (`Ctrl`+`R` / `Cmd`+`R`). If still missing after five minutes, message Apollosystems |
| Photo looks stretched or badly cropped | Open the image field, set the **Hotspot** onto the important part of the photo, publish again |
| Old example projects came back | Every real project has been deleted or unpublished. Publish one real project and they disappear |
| Cannot log in | The email invitation has not been accepted, or you are using a different email than the one invited. Message Apollosystems with the address you want to use |
| Panel will not load at all | Try a different browser. If it still fails, message Apollosystems — do not try to fix it from the panel |

---

## 8. A short checklist before you publish

- [ ] Title includes the **location**
- [ ] Status is right — and any job you have since handed over has been flipped to **Completed**
- [ ] Description is two sentences and says what was **delivered**
- [ ] Photo is landscape, daylight, and shows the **finished work**
- [ ] Hotspot set on the important part of the photo
- [ ] Two or three stats filled in
- [ ] Pressed **Publish**, not just closed the tab

---

## Support

Anything you are unsure about, message Apollosystems before changing it rather than after. Nothing in this panel is dangerous, but a finished job left marked *Ongoing* quietly makes your business look stalled.
